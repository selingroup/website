Zhanxiang combines machine learning and atmospheric science to understand and predict weather and climate extremes. At MIT, he works on the “AI-Emulators to Advance Coastal Resilience Planning” project, leveraging and developing generative climate emulators and linking them with tropical cyclone and impact models to assess coastal risks in a changing climate.

He received his PhD in Atmospheric Sciences from the University of Washington, where he studied severe convective storms and their prediction using machine learning. He then worked as a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, using a coupled atmosphere–ocean emulator to investigate ENSO and its influence on atmospheric circulation. Before joining MIT, he was a research scientist at the University of Oklahoma’s CIWRO, where he worked on inverse modeling of radar reflectivity using deep learning to improve data assimilation in MPAS-JEDI. Outside of work, he enjoys photography, traveling and gaming.
