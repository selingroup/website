Sanchit’s research lies at the intersection of human–Earth system science, artificial intelligence, and sustainability, with a focus on using advanced computational methods to address environmental challenges and support evidence-based decision-making. At MIT, he is working on B-FAST (Building a Flexible, Actionable Systems Toolkit), developing an open-source framework that integrates Earth and human systems models with AI-based emulators. As part of this project, he is exploring climate–economy feedbacks to understand how climate impacts influence socioeconomic systems and, in turn, future emissions and warming. Sanchit earned his PhD in Civil and Environmental Engineering from the University of Washington, where he combined satellite remote sensing, hydrological modeling, and machine learning to address global water resource challenges. His satellite-based reservoir monitoring framework was adopted by the Mekong River Commission as a decision-support tool. Outside of research, he enjoys swimming, badminton, reading novels, and exploring new places. For more details, visit his [personal website](https://sanchitminocha.github.io/).
